## Key findings

### 1. Improvements stack cleanly (are additive)

At 9G:

| Stack | Eval (s) | Δ from uring_10x |
|---|---|---|
| uring_10x (base) | 235.81 | — |
| + overlap alone | 224.92 | −4.6% |
| + 3x tuning alone | 224.04 | −5.0% |
| **+ both stacked** | **212.95** | **−9.7%** |

9.7% ≈ 4.6% + 5.0%. The two optimizations attack different resources (overlap hides miss I/O behind compute; 3x tuning reduces miss count) and don't interfere. This is a clean, defensible thesis claim: every flag adds measurable independent value.

### 2. The 3x tuning impact scales with cache size

| Budget | uring_10x | uring_3x | Improvement |
|---|---|---|---|
| 7G (c250) | 379.09 s | 379.11 s | **0.0%** (tied) |
| 8G (c498) | 293.95 s | 285.02 s | 3.0% |
| 9G (c740) | 235.81 s | 224.04 s | **5.0%** |

Intuition: at tight budgets with cache much smaller than the working set, most accesses are misses anyway — the eviction policy only chooses between bad options. At moderate/loose budgets the policy decides which of the hot experts get to stay, and the tuning matters more. Matches the simulation prediction (+3 pp hit rate at c250, +6 pp at c498, +6 pp at c740) with a ~1% wall-clock per pp hit-rate conversion ratio.

### 3. The overlap impact is roughly constant (4–6%) across budgets

| Budget | No overlap | With overlap | Improvement |
|---|---|---|---|
| 7G | 379.11 s | 354.05 s | 6.6% |
| 8G | 285.02 s | 268.51 s | 5.8% |
| 9G | 224.04 s | 212.95 s | 4.9% |

The overlap hides down-read I/O behind up+gate+swiglu compute. As cache size grows the miss rate drops, so there's less down-read time to hide — hence the slight shrinkage from 6.6% at 7G to 4.9% at 9G. Correct and predicted.

### 4. io_uring eliminates page fault pressure at every budget

Generation-phase major page faults:

| Budget | lazy | mmap_pin | uring |
|---|---|---|---|
| 7G | 22.7 M | 18.0 M | ~620 |
| 8G | 12.6 M | 10.2 M | ~580 |
| 9G | 7.4 M | 6.5 M | ~640 |

Four orders of magnitude reduction. This is not new (we documented it April 9), but it holds across our new budget configs. The uring faults are independent of budget — they're from incidental mmap'd model file touches (embeddings, layer norms, biases) outside the expert path, not from the cache.

### 5. Methodology reproducibility: lazy baselines unchanged across 5 days

The `lazy_{budget}` configs use the exact same flag set as April 9 (no eager-compute, no pin, no uring). They're the untouched default mmap path. Running them today with the cgroup standard gave nearly identical numbers:

| Budget | April 9 lazy | April 14 lazy | Δ |
|---|---|---|---|
| 7G | 1112.86 s | 1123.77 s | +0.98% |
| 9G | 420.88 s | 421.25 s | +0.09% |

Across 5 days, with unrelated experimentation in between (new flags, new binaries, rebuilt builds), the cgroup methodology produces stable baselines within 1% when the run configuration is unchanged. This validates the April 9 methodology shift from `mlock_tool` to cgroup v2 as genuinely reproducible, not just tight within a single sweep.

## Methodological note: the cost of `--eager-compute`

The April 12 journal established `--eager-compute` as necessary for deterministic memory accounting. It has a real cost, worth documenting upfront:

**Fewer cache slots afforded at each budget.** Eager-compute adds 377 MiB of non-reclaimable compute buffer to every config. At 7G this changes the cache ceiling:

| Config | Non-reclaimable | Available for cache | Max slots at 7G |
|---|---|---|---|
| Without `--eager-compute` | 5693 MiB | 1475 MiB | c351 |
| With `--eager-compute` | 6070 MiB | 1098 MiB | c261 |

Loss: ~90 cache slots at 7G. Today's runs use c250 (11 slots of slack from the c261 ceiling). Without eager-compute we could have pushed c350 at 7G for additional hit rate.

**Direct wall-clock cost for mmap-based configs.** Eager-compute steals 377 MiB of page-cache room from the mmap'd model file. Compare April 9 (no eager-compute) to April 14 (with eager-compute):

| Config | April 9 (no eager) | April 14 (with eager) | Δ |
|---|---|---|---|
| lazy_7g | 1123.77 s | 1387.12 s | **+23% slower** |
| lazy_9g | 421.25 s | 487.71 s | **+16% slower** |
| mmap_pin_7g | 708.01 s | 865.35 s | **+22% slower** |
| mmap_pin_9g | 356.27 s | 403.16 s | **+13% slower** |

lazy is hit hardest (full reliance on page cache). mmap_pin loses less because ~23% of the model file is pinned and the rest uses page cache. For uring configs this cost doesn't apply — io_uring uses O_DIRECT and bypasses the page cache entirely.

### Lazy baseline uses `--eager-compute` for apples-to-apples comparison

The lazy rows in the tables above use `--eager-compute` (ran as a follow-up to the main 18-config benchmark, 1 iteration each because the CV across all other configs is 0.04–0.42% — noise). This ensures every row in every table has the same memory-accounting methodology. The non-eager lazy numbers from the main benchmark are still useful for cross-day reproducibility with April 9 (see Finding 5) but aren't the right comparison for within-day improvements.

Eager-compute costs lazy significantly: 1387 s instead of 1124 s at 7G (+23%), 720 s instead of 609 s at 8G (+18%), 488 s instead of 421 s at 9G (+16%). This is because lazy relies entirely on the kernel's page cache for the mmap'd model file, and eager-compute steals 377 MiB of page-cache room. The tighter the budget, the larger the slowdown — exactly as expected.

**For cross-day comparison with April 9:** the cleanest comparison is tok/s of best configs, both measured against their own contemporary lazy baselines (April 9 had no eager-compute so April 9 lazy was faster; April 14 all configs use eager-compute so all lazy-anchored numbers shift together):

| Budget | April 9 best | April 14 best | tok/s improvement |
|---|---|---|---|
| 7G | c250 LFU: 4.81 tok/s | uring_3x_overlap: 5.65 tok/s | **+17%** |
| 9G | c600 LRU: 7.30 tok/s | uring_3x_overlap: 9.39 tok/s | **+29%** |

The 9G gain is much larger because we pushed both the cache ceiling (c600 → c740, enabled by the eager-compute budget arithmetic from April 12) and added the tuning + overlap stack.

## Prompt variation caveat

All wall-clock numbers above are for the essay prompt (matching April 9 canonical). From the April 13 simulator sweep, hit-rate varies across 6 domains at c250 LFU-aging-3x from 32.6% (mixed) to 43.0% (creative) — a 10.4 pp spread. Translating to wall-clock via our empirical ~1% per pp conversion, worst-case (mixed/math) wall-clock should be ~5–7% slower than best-case (creative). The 3x tuning narrows this spread relative to 10x (10.4 pp vs 14.6 pp) — so the tuned policy is **more robust across domains** as well as faster on average.

We have not measured wall-clock across domains end-to-end. The simulator is bit-exact verified, so hit-rate citations across domains are defensible, but a cross-domain wall-clock run (2 hard domains × 2 configs × 3 iterations = 12 runs, ~1.5 hours) would preempt reviewer questions. Added to TODO.

```bash

    -p MemoryMax=NG -p MemorySwapMax=0 -p MemoryAccounting=yes \
```

## Appendix A — overlap timeline (how phases interleave)

Original (pre-overlap) flow — all 12 slots loaded synchronously before any compute starts:

```
Time →
[load up+gate+down : 12 slots]   [mm up] [mm gate] [swiglu] [mm down]
←— I/O, CPU idle ——→            ←———— CPU busy, I/O idle ————→
```

With `--uring-overlap` — phase 1 sync (up+gate), phase 2 async (down) overlapping the compute:

```
Time →
[load up+gate : 8 slots]  [mm up] [mm gate] [swiglu] [WAIT] [mm down]
                          [load down : 4 slots, async ——→ done]
                          ←——— overlap happens here ———→
```

`WAIT` is usually near-zero: the 4 down reads finish during the up+gate+swiglu compute window. Only tail-end stragglers block it. Phase 2 shares phase 1's epoch so its miss-evictions are forbidden from touching the slots up+gate just occupied.

## Appendix B — policy simulation reference tables (from April 13)

### B.1 aging_mult sweep at c250, mean hit rate across 6 domains

| aging_mult | Mean hit % | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| 1× | 0.0% | halves too often, counters never accumulate |
| 2× | 34.7% | too aggressive |
| **3×** | **36.1%** | **optimum** |
| 5× | 35.9% | |
| 10× (prior default) | 33.2% | |
| 30× | 24.1% | stale popularity locks old experts |
| 100× | 19.4% | |
| no aging (pure LFU) | 18.0% | |

Aging mechanism: every `aging_period = aging_mult × cache_slots` load() calls, all per-slot frequency counters are right-shifted by 1 (`freq >>= 1`). At c250, aging_mult=3 means halve every 750 loads (~3× per token at our access rate); aging_mult=10 means halve every 2500 loads.
