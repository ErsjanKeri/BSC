# 13 March 2026 - Timing Instrumentation Fix & 9-Experiment Matrix

## Summary

Discovered that llama.cpp's reported `load_time_ms` is unreliable — a timer reset after model loading erases the cost of MAP_POPULATE, memory pinning, and warmup from all reported metrics. Added instrumentation to capture the true model load time. Rebuilt two binaries (prefetch ON / OFF), ran 9 experiments covering model size × memory pressure × pinning. Results in `time-tracking/results/experiment_20260312_193118/`.

## The Timer Reset Problem

Found a `llama_perf_context_reset()` call at `examples/main/completion.cpp:164` that zeros all timing counters **after** model loading but **before** inference. This means:

- MAP_POPULATE cost (24s for large model): **hidden**
- Memory pinning cost (~1.35s): **hidden**
- Warmup cost (~38s for large model): **hidden**

The reported `load_time_ms` only captures time from the reset to the end of the first prompt eval — essentially just `prompt_eval_time`. Verified with `strace` at the syscall level: prefetch ON produces `mmap(..., MAP_SHARED|MAP_POPULATE, ...)` and takes ~24s for the large model, but the reported load_time shows nothing.

### Instrumentation added

Added `[timing]` prints at key points in `llama-model.cpp` (after `init_mappings`, after `load_all_data`, after `pin_compute_weights`) and in `completion.cpp` (right before the reset). All measure elapsed time from `model.t_start_us`. New CSV columns: `init_mappings_ms`, `load_all_data_ms`, `pin_weights_ms`, `true_model_load_ms`.

### Verification (strace)

## Two Binaries

Built two versions of llama.cpp from the same instrumented source, differing only in `init_mappings(true/false, ...)` at `llama-model.cpp:6797`:

- `bin_prefetch_on/` — MAP_POPULATE enabled (out-of-the-box default)
- `bin_prefetch_off/` — MAP_POPULATE disabled (lazy mmap)

Each directory contains `llama-completion` and its own `libllama.so`. Verified with `strace` that each binary loads the correct library.

## Results

All values are means of valid runs (199 tokens generated). Wall clock = `true_model_load` + `total_time` (captures costs hidden by the reset).

### No pressure experiments

| # | Config | Wall clock | tok/s | True load | Reported load | Page faults |
|---|--------|-----------|-------|-----------|---------------|-------------|
| 1 | Small default (gold) | **19.4s** | **15.20** | 6,101ms | 6,101ms | 142 |
| 2 | Large default | **99.4s** | **6.18** | 63,147ms | 63,147ms | 813,760 |
| 3 | Large lazy | **37.0s** | **6.17** | 230ms | 4,302ms | 511,527 |
| 4 | Large lazy + pinned | **37.1s** | **6.27** | 1,351ms | 3,703ms | 478,462 |

- Exp 2 vs 3: removing prefetch+warmup cuts wall clock from 99.4s to 37.0s (2.7x). Token generation speed identical (~6.2 tok/s). The 62s saved is pure wasted I/O from MAP_POPULATE reading 61 GiB that can't fit in 30 GiB RAM.
- Exp 3 vs 4: pinning adds 1.12s true load cost (reading+locking 2.98 GiB compute weights). Reported load drops by 600ms (3,703 vs 4,302ms) — but this is the measurement artifact we discovered: pinning pre-faults compute weights, making prompt eval faster, but the pinning cost is hidden before the reset. Wall clock is identical (37.0 vs 37.1s). Net effect: **zero**.
- Exp 1 vs 2: for default config, init_mappings takes 4.9s (small, 13 GiB) vs 23.6s (large, 61 GiB) — this is MAP_POPULATE reading the model file. Large model also has 39.5s of warmup (all 128 experts activated).

### Under 15 GiB pressure (15 GiB free RAM)

| # | Config | Wall clock | tok/s | True load | Reported load | Page faults |
|---|--------|-----------|-------|-----------|---------------|-------------|
| 5 | Large lazy | **62.5s** | **3.51** | 230ms | 5,418ms | 1,259,864 |
| 6 | Large lazy + pinned | **62.4s** | **3.55** | 1,381ms | 4,816ms | 1,240,135 |
| 7 | Small default | **27.1s** | **14.71** | 12,866ms | 12,866ms | 91,457 |
| 8 | Small lazy | **16.8s** | **13.97** | 228ms | 2,307ms | 68,701 |
| 9 | Small lazy + pinned | **17.3s** | **13.95** | 1,132ms | 1,846ms | 55,884 |

- Exp 5 vs 6 (large + pressure): pinning has negligible effect. Wall clock identical (62.5 vs 62.4s), tok/s identical (3.51 vs 3.55). Under severe pressure (4x model-to-RAM ratio), pinning 2.98 GiB out of 61 GiB is a drop in the bucket — and it steals page cache from the 57 GiB of expert weights that need it.
- Exp 8 vs 9 (small + pressure): pinning slightly slower overall (17.3 vs 16.8s) despite fewer page faults (55,884 vs 68,701). The 1.13s pinning cost eats the page fault savings. Token speed identical (13.95 vs 13.97).
- Exp 7 (small default + pressure): MAP_POPULATE still reads the full 13 GiB (init_mappings = 4,956ms), then warmup adds another 7.9s. Total true_model_load = 12.9s. Under pressure, defaults hurt startup badly.
- **15 GiB pressure is insufficient for the small model.** With 15 GiB free and a 13 GiB model, it still mostly fits in RAM. Exp 8 tok/s = 13.97 vs Exp 1 gold standard = 15.20 — only 8% degradation. Need 20-25 GiB pressure for meaningful differentiation.

## Key Findings

1. **The timer reset is the root cause of misleading load_time measurements in all prior experiments.** Every optimization cost (MAP_POPULATE, warmup, pinning) was invisible in reported metrics. Our `true_model_load_ms` instrumentation fixes this.

2. **Memory pinning (`--pin-compute-weights`) provides no measurable benefit** in any configuration we tested — not at 2x model-to-RAM ratio, not at 4x. The compute weights (4.8% of model) are too small a fraction to matter, and pinning them steals page cache from the 93.4% expert weights.

3. **The "load_time improvement" from pinning was a measurement artifact.** Pinning pre-faults compute weights before the reset, making post-reset prompt eval faster. But the pinning cost itself is hidden. True wall clock shows no improvement.

4. **For the large model, lazy loading (no prefetch, no warmup) remains the clear winner.** 99.4s → 37.0s with identical token generation speed.

## Next Steps

- Consider whether pinning could help with a different model architecture (non-MoE, where compute weights are a larger fraction)
