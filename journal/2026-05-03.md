## The 120B OOM and what we learned

On May 1, the 120B half of `settings_thesis_final.json` killed all 48 uring cells (every config at every budget) with cgroup OOM during decode. The lazy and mmap_pin 120B cells finished cleanly. The 20B half (96 runs) finished cleanly. Only the 120B uring cells died.

Root cause: the cache_slots ceiling formula I had applied was the 20B canonical formula (April 12), with `fixed_anon = 6070 MiB` based on the 20B numbers `2329 pin + 3164 KV + 413 compute + 108 misc`. I scaled `pin` and `KV` to 120B (2980 + 4635 + 413), but I left `misc` at 108 MiB. On 120B, "misc anon" actually measures 232 MiB, not 108 MiB, because libgomp's threadpool, ggml's per-thread scratch buffers, the io_uring submission and completion rings, and the bsc-pack staging buffers all scale with `n_layer = 36` and `n_expert = 128`. The undercount was 124 MiB plus a separate 71 MiB shortfall in the pin estimate (actual 3051 MiB vs estimated 2980 MiB), giving a net 195 MiB underbudget. With `cache_slots = 977` at the tightest 12 G cell, this put the runtime exactly 149 MiB over the cgroup ceiling at decode iter 2. OOM.

Eager-compute did NOT cause the OOM. The earlier "ceilings derived in April 12" were correct for 20B; they were never re-derived for 120B with measured 120B numbers. The user's question "I thought eager-compute made things easier?" is fair — eager-compute makes the budget DETERMINISTIC (the 413 MiB compute buffer becomes a deterministic non-evictable footprint instead of a lazy-faulted ~36 MiB), but it does not save memory. It just makes the math honest. The OOM was failure to redo the math for the bigger model.

The fix for the May 2 retry was to derive `fixed_anon` from a measured 120B smoke run (`/tmp/smoke_120b_20260501_185202/log_async_experts.txt`, decode iter 50 steady state): KV 4635 + compute 413 + misc 232 = 5280 MiB. With a 1 GiB transient safety margin and 4-slot defensive margin, the formula becomes `cache_slots = floor((budget − 3051 pin − 5280 fixed_anon − 1024 safety) / 4.203 slot_bytes) − 4`. This yields c693 / c1668 / c3130 / c4592 for 12 G / 16 G / 22 G / 28 G respectively. All 72 retry runs completed without OOM. cgroup `memory.peak` ranged from 12,283 MiB (12 G run) to 28,540 MiB (28 G run), comfortably under their respective limits.

Methodology takeaway, recorded for future model swaps: **`fixed_anon` is a per-model, per-binary-version measurement. It is not portable from one model to another, even with the same flag set.** The April 12 entry's "108 MiB misc" was specific to 20B + that day's binary; it was never a general constant. From now on, any model swap or binary-revision change must include a smoke run that produces the actual `fixed_anon`. The smoke script is `/tmp/smoke_120b.sh`, generalizable.

## Conservation law generalizes

The May 1 entry argued that ae's wall-clock disadvantage vs apo is structural: the total synchronous-I/O-wait by thread 0 is a function of `(disk read latency) - (parallel compute that overlaps it)`, and changing dispatch granularity does not change this product. The May 2 120B data is consistent with this: ae loses to apo at every cell, the loss is 0.9 to 1.7%, and it shrinks as compute-per-action grows (i.e., it shrinks at 120B vs 20B at the same cache_slots-relative-to-WS regime). Two independent models, two independent compute scales, same direction, same magnitude band. The thesis can present this as a measured negative result generalizable across the model sizes we have data for.

I did not redo the picker decomposition instrumentation (`ae_t0_pick_compute_ms`, `ae_t0_pick_io_wait_ms`) on the 120B sweep because (a) it would have required a non-canonical iter setup and (b) the conservation-law mechanism documented May 1 already suffices. The 20B numbers from May 1 remain the load-bearing mechanism evidence; the 120B numbers from May 2-3 are the cross-model corroboration.

## Memory budget arithmetic, definitive

For future model swaps and for thesis text, the per-model budget breakdown that the May 2 sweep validates:

**20B at any cgroup memory.max = NG (where N ≥ 6 G):**
- `pin_compute_weights` mlocked: 2329 MiB
- `KV self size` (ctx=131072): 3164 MiB
- `compute buffer` (eager-faulted): 413 MiB
- `misc anon` (libgomp + ggml scratch + uring rings + pack staging): 108 MiB
- **fixed footprint: 6014 MiB**
- mmap'd model file working set: variable, fills remaining headroom up to ~12.85 GiB
- io_uring expert cache (anonymous, configurable): `cache_slots × 4.203 MiB`
- safety margin: 1024 MiB transient + 4 slots × 4.203 MiB ≈ 17 MiB defensive
- **cache_slots ceiling = floor((N × 1024 − 6014 − 1024) / 4.203) − 4**
- Yields c250 / c498 / c742 at 7G / 8G / 9G respectively, matching April 12 derivation.

**120B at any cgroup memory.max = NG (where N ≥ 11 G):**
- `pin_compute_weights` mlocked: 3051 MiB
- `KV self size` (ctx=131072): 4635 MiB
- `compute buffer` (eager-faulted): 413 MiB
- `misc anon` (scaled to n_layer=36, n_expert=128): 232 MiB
- **fixed footprint: 8331 MiB**
- mmap'd model file working set: variable, fills remaining headroom up to ~65.4 GiB (so always pressure for budgets ≤ 30 GiB)
- io_uring expert cache: `cache_slots × 4.203 MiB`
- **cache_slots ceiling = floor((N × 1024 − 8331 − 1024) / 4.203) − 4**
- Yields c693 / c1668 / c3130 / c4592 at 12G / 16G / 22G / 28G respectively.

The ratio 120B-fixed-footprint / 20B-fixed-footprint = 8331/6014 = 1.385x. About what you would expect from a model with 2.2x the weights but the same context length (KV scales with n_layer × n_head × ctx, not n_total_params).

## Caveats not yet validated

In rough priority order:

1. **One day per model.** 20B numbers are from May 1; 120B numbers are from May 2 (lazy/mmap_pin had a same-day cross-check on May 1 but the uring cells are May 2 only). Cross-day reproducibility was validated on the lazy/mmap_pin baselines; it was NOT validated on the uring cells. If a uring config drifts day-to-day in a way the baselines do not, that drift would not show in this sweep.
2. **One workload.** The multi-topic essay prompt is the realistic stand-in; we did not measure other genres (code, math, dialogue). Cross-genre wall-clock variance is likely small (router behavior is similar) but unmeasured.
3. **Conservation-law instrumentation only at 20B.** The picker-decomposition counters (`ae_t0_pick_compute_ms`, `ae_t0_pick_io_wait_ms`) added May 1 were not surfaced through the 120B sweep's run wrapper. The 120B apo > ae result is consistent with the conservation law, but the mechanism evidence is 20B-only.
4. **No 22 G measurement of pipeline at 20B with all 6 configs.** We have lazy/mmap_pin/po/apo/ae at 20B 22 G but not the policy sweep. At 22 G the policy choice is irrelevant (the cache fits everything), so this is not a real gap.
5. **120B 28 G is inside-budget but tight on the safety margin.** c4592 leaves about 1024 MiB safety; cgroup peak measured 28,540 MiB on the worst run vs 28,672 limit. If the kernel's reclaim pressure ever spikes by more than 130 MiB at decode iter X, this cell could OOM. 3 iters × 6 configs × 28 G = 18 runs all completed cleanly, so the empirical safety margin is ≥1 standard run. Not validated for ≥ 24-hour or ≥ 100-run stability.
6. **The OOM lesson is documented but not defended in the thesis text yet.** The key methodological point is "fixed_anon is per-model, not portable from another model's number". Worth a paragraph in the thesis methodology section.

The thesis can now cite:
- A unified table of 8 budget × model rows × 5-9 configs.
- A clean "apo wins" headline at every (model, budget) cell where pressure exists.
- A clean "ae has a measurable structural disadvantage" finding at both models.
- Cross-day reproducibility on the lazy/mmap_pin baselines for both models.
- A documented OOM and corrected budget arithmetic that future-proofs the methodology against the next model swap.

What it should NOT cite without an additional run:
- An exact apo-vs-ae percentage as the headline number for either model. The directional finding is solid; the exact gap is one-day-one-machine.
- 22 G uring numbers as if they were a regime where uring helps. They are the regime where uring loses by 0.6 to 2.6% because there is no I/O to hide.
